Public Speaking — Overview

What is Public Speaking?

Public speaking is the act of delivering prepared or extemporaneous oral messages to audiences. It combines content knowledge, organizational skills, verbal and nonverbal delivery techniques, and audience engagement strategies to inform, persuade, entertain, or commemorate.

This overview provides essential basics. For comprehensive coverage including classical rhetoric, persuasion theory, anxiety management, and advanced techniques, use the "In-Depth" link below.

Core Elements of Effective Speaking

1. The Rhetorical Triangle

  • Ethos (Credibility): Establish your expertise and trustworthiness
  • Pathos (Emotion): Connect with audience values and feelings
  • Logos (Logic): Use sound reasoning and evidence

2. Speech Structure

  • Introduction (10-15%): Attention-getter, credibility, thesis, preview main points
  • Body (70-80%): 2-5 main points with supporting evidence and clear transitions
  • Conclusion (10-15%): Review points, restate thesis, memorable closing

3. Delivery Essentials

  • Verbal: Clear articulation, varied pace and tone, strategic pauses, avoid filler words
  • Nonverbal: Eye contact, natural gestures, good posture, confident movement
  • Voice: Appropriate volume, pitch variation, controlled rate (120-150 wpm)

Types of Speeches

  • Informative: Educate audience on a topic (lectures, demonstrations, reports)
  • Persuasive: Change beliefs, attitudes, or behaviors (advocacy, sales pitches)
  • Ceremonial: Commemorate or celebrate (toasts, eulogies, awards)
  • Entertaining: Amuse or inspire through storytelling and humor

Quick Preparation Checklist

  • ✓ Analyze your audience: demographics, knowledge level, interests
  • ✓ Craft clear thesis: one central message
  • ✓ Organize logically: intro, 2-5 main points, conclusion
  • ✓ Support with evidence: statistics, examples, expert testimony
  • ✓ Create strong opening: hook attention immediately
  • ✓ Practice aloud: rehearse multiple times, time yourself
  • ✓ Prepare visual aids: simple, clear, readable slides if needed
  • ✓ Plan memorable close: call to action or powerful quote

Managing Nervousness

Remember: Most speakers feel nervous—it's normal and manageable.

  • Practice thoroughly to build confidence
  • Use deep breathing before speaking to calm nerves
  • Focus on your message, not yourself
  • Make eye contact with friendly faces first
  • Reframe nervousness as excitement and energy
  • Remember the audience wants you to succeed

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Reading slides word-for-word instead of speaking conversationally
  • Including too many points (causes information overload)
  • Lack of eye contact or staring at notes/screen
  • Speaking in monotone without vocal variety
  • Starting with apologies or weak openings
  • Going over time or rushing through content
  • Ignoring audience reactions and feedback
  • Using jargon without explanation

Quick Tips for Success

Before Speaking

  • • Know your material inside and out
  • • Arrive early to check venue and tech
  • • Do vocal warm-ups
  • • Visualize success

During Speaking

  • • Start with confidence and energy
  • • Pause for emphasis and processing
  • • Adapt to audience reactions
  • • End strong—don't trail off
