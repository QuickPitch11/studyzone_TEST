MLA Essays: In Depth Analysis

Formatting, in-text citations, Works Cited entries, sample paragraphs, and a checklist for MLA (9th edition guidance)

MLA Quick Facts

  • Edition: Follow MLA 8/9 conventions (most instructors accept MLA 8 or 9).
  • General format: 1-inch margins, double-spaced, legible 12-pt font (Times New Roman commonly used), no extra spacing between paragraphs.
  • Header: Student name, instructor, course, and date appear on the first page (unless instructor requests a title page). Page numbers and author last name in the header at top-right are optional in some styles — instructors vary.
  • In-text citations: Author–page style: (Smith 23).
  • Works Cited: Title "Works Cited" centered; entries listed alphabetically by author; use hanging indent.

How to Write an MLA Essay — Practical Steps

  1. Plan & Thesis: Draft a clear thesis; keep it focused and arguable.
  2. Research: Gather reliable literary sources, books, and articles; record full bibliographic info.
  3. Outline: Use topic sentences, evidence, and analysis for each paragraph.
  4. Write: Integrate quotations and paraphrases with author–page citations.
  5. Format Works Cited: Build each entry with MLA ordering and punctuation; alphabetize the list.
  6. Proofread & Style: Ensure consistent tense, voice, and correct citation usage.

In-Text Citation Examples

  • Paraphrase (author in parentheses): The study suggests a correlation between X and Y (Johnson 45).
  • Narrative (author named): Johnson argues that X increases Y (45).
  • Multiple authors: (Garcia and Lee 102) for two authors; (Martin et al. 78) for three or more.
  • Quotations: Short quote: "..." (Smith 23). Block quote (more than four lines): start on new line, indented 0.5 in, no quotation marks, citation after punctuation.

Works Cited — Common Formats

Book: Lastname, Firstname. Title of Book. Publisher, Year.

Example: Smith, John. Research Methods in Education. Academic Press, 2018.

Journal article: Lastname, Firstname. "Title of Article." Journal Title, vol. 12, no. 3, Year, pp. 45-60.

Example: Lee, Carmen, and Sarah Park. "Sleep and Cognition." Journal of Sleep Research, vol. 29, no. 3, 2020, pp. 1-10.

Web page: Lastname, Firstname (or Organization). "Title of Page." Website Title, Day Month Year, URL. Accessed Day Month Year (optional).

Example: World Health Organization. "Coronavirus Disease (COVID-19) Advice." WHO, 5 Mar. 2021, https://www.who.int/...

Sample Works Cited

Smith, John. Research Methods in Education. Academic Press, 2018.

Lee, Carmen, and Sarah Park. "Sleep and Cognition." Journal of Sleep Research, vol. 29, no. 3, 2020, pp. 1-10.

Brown, Lisa. "Teaching Online." Online Pedagogy, edited by Rachel Green, 2nd ed., Routledge, 2019, pp. 45-67.

World Health Organization. "Coronavirus Disease (COVID-19) Advice." WHO, 5 Mar. 2021, https://www.who.int/.

Nolan, Christopher, director. Inception. Warner Bros., 2010.

TEDx Talks. "The Power of Introverts." YouTube, uploaded by TEDx Talks, 12 Mar. 2018, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EXAMPLE.

Notes: alphabetize entries by author surname; apply a hanging indent (0.5 in) for each Works Cited entry in your document.

Sample Paragraph (MLA style)

Topic sentence: The prevalence of social media has changed how young adults form political opinions.

Evidence + citation: Recent surveys indicate that exposure to algorithmic feeds increases the likelihood of echo-chamber effects (Garcia and Lee 134).

Analysis: This suggests platforms amplify pre-existing beliefs; educators should teach critical digital literacy to mitigate polarization.

Common Mistakes & Fixes

  • Forgetting author–page format — include page numbers where available.
  • Using the wrong order or punctuation in Works Cited — follow MLA examples closely.
  • Not using hanging indent — apply a 0.5 in hanging indent for each Works Cited entry.

Quick Submission Checklist

  • Double-spaced, 1-inch margins, readable 12-pt font.
  • Header or title per instructor requirements; no extra spaces.
  • In-text citations use author–page style and match Works Cited entries.
  • Works Cited page titled and alphabetized with hanging indents.
  • Proofread for grammar, clarity, and proper quotation formatting.
